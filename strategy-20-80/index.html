Research Problem · Prepared by Sagi Simhi

How does incorporating a small-cap value factor tilt impact the risk-return characteristics of a globally diversified equity portfolio?

This case study examines the construction of an 80/20 core-satellite portfolio combining broad global equity exposure with a targeted small-cap value factor tilt. The analysis evaluates whether this factor-based approach alters portfolio characteristics in meaningful ways compared to traditional market-capitalization weighted approaches.

Research Context

Why Factor-Based portfolio construction?

Decades of academic research in financial economics have identified systematic factors that help explain differences in security returns. The Fama-French three-factor model, extending the traditional Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM), demonstrates that size (SMB) and value (HML) factors, alongside the market factor, provide a more complete explanation of equity returns than market beta alone. This research builds upon that foundation to evaluate how incorporating these insights impacts portfolio construction.

Research Foundation

This analysis is grounded in the Fama-French factor framework, which has shown that size and value factors have historically provided explanatory power for equity returns beyond market exposure alone.

Factor Definition

The small-cap value factor targets companies with smaller market capitalizations and lower valuations relative to fundamentals, based on the size (SMB) and value (HML) factors from the Fama-French model.

To evaluate the impact of these factors, this study employs a simulated approach due to limited historical data availability for the specific ETFs used as proxies (FTSE All-World and AVGS).

Analytical Approach

Regime-Based Return Modeling

To evaluate factor tilt impact given limited historical ETF data, this study employs a calibrated regime-based return model that simulates 240 months (Jan 2005 – Dec 2024) of monthly returns across asset classes. The approach captures varying market conditions through distinct regimes, allowing analysis of how factor performance changes across different market environments.

Market

Market Factor Exposure

Beta coefficient measuring sensitivity to overall market returns, representing systematic risk exposure common to all equities.

SMB

Size Factor Exposure

Beta coefficient measuring sensitivity to returns of small-cap versus large-cap stocks, capturing the size premium documented in financial literature.

HML

Value Factor Exposure

Beta coefficient measuring sensitivity to returns of high book-to-market (value) versus low book-to-market (growth) stocks, capturing the value premium.

The model uses synthetic returns calibrated to approximate realistic long-term equity characteristics. Results illustrate potential behavior under modeled conditions and do not represent actual historical performance of any tradable security.

Model Assumptions

Return Model Parameters

To evaluate factor performance across different market conditions, this analysis uses a regime-based return model that captures varying market environments. The model consists of five distinct regimes representing different economic periods, each with specific market drift, volatility, and factor premium assumptions.

Market Regimes

The model incorporates five regimes: Bull markets (low volatility), Bear markets (high volatility), Recovery periods, Stagflation environments, and Moderate growth periods, each calibrated to historical equity market characteristics.

Asset Sensitivities

Each asset class (Global Equity Core, MSCI ACWI, S&P 500, Small-Cap Value) is defined by its beta (market sensitivity), beta-value (factor sensitivity), alpha (excess return), and idiosyncratic volatility parameters.

The simulation uses a fixed seed for reproducibility and generates 240 monthly returns (20 years) to ensure consistent analysis across different factor tilt allocations.

Analytical Framework

How Factor Exposure Is Measured

To evaluate the impact of factor tilts, this analysis measures portfolio sensitivity to three key dimensions: market exposure (beta), size factor exposure (SMB beta), and value factor exposure (HML beta). This multi-factor approach allows decomposition of returns into systematic components driven by each underlying factor.

Risk Disclosure

Where This Strategy May Underperform

A credible portfolio thesis accounts for how and when it can fail. The following are known, structural risks of this approach.

!

Extended Value Underperformance

Value stocks have historically gone through multi-year periods of lagging growth stocks, sometimes lasting a decade or more.

!

Small-Cap Volatility

Smaller companies tend to exhibit higher volatility and deeper drawdowns than large, established firms.

!

Tracking Error vs. Popular Benchmarks

Deviating from cap-weighted benchmarks like the S&P 500 means periods of underperformance relative to widely followed indices.

!

Psychological Difficulty

Maintaining a factor tilt through a multi-year underperformance cycle requires discipline that is harder to sustain in practice than in theory.

!

Premiums May Weaken

Increased awareness and crowding into known factors may compress future premiums. Historical patterns are not assured to persist.
