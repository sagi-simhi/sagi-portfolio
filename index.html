How I Think About Systems

My Approach

I bridge Economics and Computer Science through systems thinking — understanding how data flows through a business, where signals get lost, and how to surface the information that actually drives decisions.

1. Frame the Question

Before touching data, I define the decision the analysis needs to support and the metric that would change the answer. This systems-first approach comes from my operational background where the wrong question, asked quickly, was worse than no question at all.

2. Map the Data

I trace the data from its source — what it measures, where it's logged, what's missing, and how reliable it is. Drawing from Economics, I look for the incentive structure behind the data: what the system is actually optimizing, and where that distorts the numbers.

3. Query, Model, and Visualize

Using SQL, Python, and Excel, I pull the data, build a structured model, and present it in a way that surfaces the answer quickly. The Computer Science half of the degree gives me the rigor to keep the pipeline reproducible, not a one-off spreadsheet.

4. Hand It Off Clearly

An analysis only matters if the next person can act on it. I write up the finding with the assumption stated, the data quality flagged, and the decision framed — then ship it as a dashboard or a short brief, not a 30-page report.
